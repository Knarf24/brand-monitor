📣 Brand Reputation Monitor

Small businesses rarely know what people say about them online, and checking every news site and forum by hand takes hours. Type a brand, product or name and this app gathers what news sites, Reddit, Hacker News and Mastodon said about it, scores each mention by mood and tells you what people praise, what they complain about, and whether complaints are rising.

🐍 The reading and mood scoring is Python (VADER sentiment) running right here in your browser with Pyodide. No account, no paid services, nothing stored on a server.

        How it works

        1. Search. The page asks free public services that allow other websites to read them: GDELT (news sites worldwide), Reddit, Hacker News and Mastodon (for one-word names, the matching #hashtag).
        2. Read. Python (monitor.py) turns each reply into the same simple "mention" list, drops repeats and posts that don't actually name what you typed.
        3. Score. Each mention gets a mood from −1 to +1 with VADER, a sentiment model built for social media, plus business words it doesn't know (recall, lawsuit, outage, layoffs, soars, award…).
        4. Sum up. Mood score 0 to 100 (50 = neutral), share of positive / neutral / negative mentions, mood per source and per day, the words people use when they praise or complain, and an early warning when complaints rise or fall in the last 24 hours.

        Honest notes

        • X (Twitter) is not included: its API is paid and it blocks free reading. Paste posts into "Score your own text" instead. Mastodon is included as a free alternative. Bluesky's search now needs a login, so it is left out.
        • Reddit often refuses requests from servers and sometimes from browsers too. When it does, the other sources still count.
        • Some services limit how often a browser may ask. If one fails, the others still count, and tracked brands fall back to the last scheduled check.
        • Mood scoring is English only and reads words, not sarcasm. Treat it as a quick pulse, then read the mentions behind it.
        • News is scored on the headline only.
